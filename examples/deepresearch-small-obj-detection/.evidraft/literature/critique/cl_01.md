## yuan2023cfinet — CFINet (ICCV 2023) — **primary contrast**

**Mechanism (from abstract).** Two-stage detector. (1) Coarse-to-fine RPN with dynamic anchor selection + cascade regression ⇒ dense, high-quality proposals for small objects. (2) Detection head augmented with a **Feature Imitation (FI) branch** trained via a supervised contrastive `auxiliary imitation loss` to enhance representations of size-limited instances. Integrated with Faster R-CNN. SOTA on SODA-D / SODA-A.

- **Strengths.**
  - Feature Imitation branch IS the same architectural trick we proposed (aux branch + Faster R-CNN). [TODO: PDF — confirm whether FI branch is dropped at inference or kept]
  - Small-object focus is explicit (SODA-D/A are small-object benchmarks).
  - Faster R-CNN integration validated.
- **Weaknesses.** [TODO: PDF]
  - Two-stage at inference (Faster R-CNN baseline) — inference cost is not the headline.
  - Supervision signal is contrastive (paired features), not region-aware.
- **Opportunities (vs ours).**
  - Their supervision signal trains the *features* to imitate; ours trains *which regions* to attend (heatmap / density). Different inductive bias.
- **Threats (to our novelty).**
  - If the FI branch is in fact dropped at inference, the architectural distinction collapses; only the supervision signal remains as differentiator.
- **Delta vs our angle.**
  - **Our defensible distinctions are now narrower than originally framed**: (i) inference-time single-stage detector (CFINet stays two-stage); (ii) supervision signal is small-object-region heatmap / density (CFINet's is feature-level contrastive); (iii) supervision applied to ROI head, not feature embedding. Items (i)+(iii) together are a real engineering distinction; item (ii) is a separate axis worth ablating.

## clab2025 — CLAB (arXiv 2025) — **mechanism-cite only (VOD, caveat)**

**Mechanism (from abstract).** Contrastive auxiliary branch via contrastive loss enhances backbone features for *video* object detection. The branch is **discarded during inference**; no extra inference cost.

- **Strengths.** Explicit confirmation that "aux branch dropped at inference" is now an active pattern with proven results.
- **Weaknesses.** Video modality (not our setting). Backbone-level supervision, not head-level.
- **Delta vs our angle.** We cite CLAB in *Method* as evidence the train-vs-inference asymmetry generalises. We do **not** compare numbers (different task family). This is the screening row tagged `include_with_caveat`.

## mrdetr2024 — Mr. DETR (arXiv 2024)

**Mechanism (from abstract).** Multi-route training for DETR-family detectors. Route-1 and Route-3 are auxiliary one-to-many prediction routes; they are dropped at inference; the deployed model architecture and inference time are unchanged.

- **Strengths.** Same train-vs-inference asymmetry pattern in a different detection-head family.
- **Weaknesses.** DETR-family; our baseline is Faster R-CNN. Not directly comparable on FPS.
- **Delta vs our angle.** Cite as evidence the trick is mainstream in DETR. Argue: applying it to the *RPN side* of a Faster-R-CNN-class detector is what's missing.

## rtdetrv3_2024 — RT-DETRv3 (arXiv 2024)

**Mechanism (from abstract).** Hierarchical dense positive supervision: multiple auxiliary branches at training, no inference latency.

- **Strengths.** Same pattern as Mr. DETR — independent evidence the asymmetry pattern is robust.
- **Weaknesses.** DETR-family again; supervision is dense-positive sampling, not region-aware.
- **Delta vs our angle.** Same as Mr. DETR. Cite together as a "DETR side has this trick; we extend to Faster R-CNN with a different supervision signal" framing.
