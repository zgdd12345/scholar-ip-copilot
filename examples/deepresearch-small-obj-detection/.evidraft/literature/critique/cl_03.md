# Cluster cl_03: Inference-time scale handling (geometric slicing)

> run_id: dr-2026-05-18-002

## akyon2022sahi — SAHI (IEEE ICIP 2022)

**Mechanism (from abstract).** Two parts. (1) **Slicing-aided inference**: slice the input image into overlapping patches, run the base detector per patch, merge detections. (2) **Slicing-aided fine-tuning**: enlarge training data by extracting patches and resizing to larger images.

**Reported numbers.**
- Inference-only: +6.8 / +5.1 / +5.3 AP on FCOS / VFNet / TOOD.
- + fine-tuning: cumulative +12.7 / +13.4 / +14.5 AP.

- **Strengths.**
  - Generic — works on top of any detector without modifying its weights (inference-only mode).
  - Open-source, integrated with Detectron2 / MMDetection / YOLOv5.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - Inference cost scales with `slices × detector_FLOPs` — significant overhead at deployment.
  - No learning signal at training time (in inference-only mode); the fine-tuning variant breaks this.
- **Opportunities (vs ours).**
  - SAHI is the strongest "no training change" baseline. Our paper claims small-object improvement without paying SAHI's inference cost — this is the cleanest accuracy/cost comparison.
- **Threats (to our novelty).**
  - On absolute accuracy, SAHI + fine-tuning's +14.5 AP is hard to beat without a learned mechanism.
- **Delta vs our angle.**
  - **SAHI moves cost to inference; we move it to training.** Standard contrast in any small-obj paper.

## Cluster summary

Single member. SAHI must appear in every small-obj paper's related work; the canonical positioning is "inference-time geometric scheme that any detector can wrap, at the cost of inference time." Our framing: ours is intrinsic, not post-hoc.
