# Cluster cl_02: Coarse-to-fine / cascaded refinement for small objects

## yang2022querydet — QueryDet (CVPR 2022)

**Mechanism (from abstract).** Two-stage pipeline: (1) predict coarse locations of small objects on low-resolution features; (2) compute accurate detections using high-resolution features **sparsely guided** by the coarse positions. Avoids dense computation on background.

**Reported numbers.**
- COCO: +1.0 mAP, +2.0 mAP_small over baseline.
- VisDrone: new SOTA + 2.3× high-resolution acceleration.

- **Strengths.**
  - Concrete VisDrone improvement (closest direct comparator).
  - Sparsity-on-high-res is a clean, motivated mechanism.
- **Weaknesses.** [TODO: PDF]
  - Sparse-query module is alive at inference (the speedup is via sparsity, not via dropping the module).
  - Coarse-prediction head is a fixed architectural choice; not learnable region prior.
- **Opportunities (vs ours).**
  - Both QueryDet and ours target "spend computation where small objects actually are." Ours pushes the cost to training (aux branch); QueryDet pushes it to inference (sparse compute). The two are not mutually exclusive — combining them is a plausible v2.
- **Threats (to our novelty).**
  - On VisDrone, QueryDet's accuracy/speed Pareto may already dominate what we can achieve with the aux-only approach. Need head-to-head VisDrone numbers in our experiments.
- **Delta vs our angle.**
  - **QueryDet keeps both stages at inference; we keep one.** That is the headline difference. Our claim: "matched-FPS to a single-stage detector with comparable small-object accuracy to QueryDet two-stage."

## Cluster summary

Single-member cluster; QueryDet defines the family. CFINet's coarse-to-fine RPN belongs structurally here but the FI auxiliary branch is the dominant feature, so it lives in `cl_01`. If QueryDet's high-res-acceleration result is robust, our paper needs an explicit speed-vs-accuracy curve against it.
